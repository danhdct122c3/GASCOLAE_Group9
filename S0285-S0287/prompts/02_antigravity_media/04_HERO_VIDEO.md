# Prompt 04 — Dynamic Multi-Stage Hero Video

Use the approved hero image as the primary visual reference and starting frame.

Generate an 8-second seamless looping website hero video in 16:9 at 1920x1080. Create one continuous cinematic camera move containing four visually distinct stages. Do not use abrupt cuts.

OVERALL PURPOSE

Show a technically credible UAV LiDAR forest-survey mission progressing from real forest observation to LiDAR point-cloud acquisition and geospatial carbon mapping.

The video must communicate movement across a programmed flight transect. The UAV must fly forward with clear relative velocity against the forest. It must never appear to hover in one fixed position.

TIMELINE AND SHOT PROGRESSION

STAGE 1 — UAV APPROACH
Time: 0.0–2.0 seconds

Begin with a low forward-tracking aerial view above a dense Southeast Asian tropical forest in clear morning daylight.

An industrial multirotor UAV enters from the upper-right area and flies diagonally forward toward the center-right. The aircraft remains entirely within the right 58 percent of the frame.

The UAV has a subtle forward pitch of approximately 4–6 degrees. Its rotors show natural motion blur while the body and downward-facing LiDAR sensor remain sharp and geometrically stable.

Create strong but realistic parallax:
- nearby treetops move faster beneath the camera;
- distant canopy moves more slowly;
- the UAV has visible forward motion relative to both layers.

Do not simulate movement using only a global camera zoom.

STAGE 2 — ACTIVE FLIGHT TRANSECT
Time: 2.0–4.0 seconds

Transition into a side-rear tracking angle without cutting.

The camera tracks alongside and slightly behind the moving UAV. The aircraft continues flying forward along a visible survey transect.

A restrained cyan and lime LiDAR cone originates precisely from the downward-facing sensor pod attached beneath the UAV.

The apex of the scan must remain locked to the sensor position at every frame.

The scan footprint moves continuously across the forest canopy with the UAV. It forms a moderately wide survey swath rather than a narrow spotlight.

The surveyed area remains visible behind the UAV as a progressive data trail:
- natural canopy before scanning;
- active cyan/lime points beneath the sensor;
- processed 3D point cloud behind the flight path.

The scan must clearly communicate continuous area coverage.

STAGE 3 — POINT-CLOUD REVEAL
Time: 4.0–6.0 seconds

The camera performs a smooth 15–20 degree orbit around the right side of the UAV while gradually gaining altitude.

Maintain the same UAV, rotor count, sensor position, lighting and flight direction.

As the camera moves, the surveyed forest swath transitions progressively into a scientifically plausible three-dimensional LiDAR point cloud.

Reveal:
- upper canopy structure;
- individual tree-height variation;
- lower vegetation;
- partial terrain structure beneath the canopy.

Use carbon cyan #64D8C1 and signal lime #9CF06A for newly captured points. Use data blue #287A96 for processed data.

The point cloud should appear because the moving scan has collected data, not as an unrelated visual effect.

STAGE 4 — GEOSPATIAL MAP AND LOOP RETURN
Time: 6.0–8.0 seconds

Continue the same camera movement toward a near top-down survey view.

The point cloud resolves smoothly into a clean geospatial visualization:
- forest plot boundaries;
- subtle canopy-height variation;
- restrained carbon-distribution color zones;
- the completed survey swath aligned with the UAV flight path.

Do not display text, legends, values, measurements or fabricated accuracy data.

During the final second, allow the geospatial overlay to dissolve gradually back into the natural forest canopy.

Guide the camera toward the same viewing direction, altitude impression, forest texture, lighting and motion vector used at the beginning.

The final frame must connect naturally to the opening frame. Match the first and final frames in:
- camera direction;
- overall canopy brightness;
- horizon orientation;
- color balance;
- motion direction.

The UAV may briefly pass beyond the right edge near the end so it can re-enter naturally when the loop restarts.

COMPOSITION AND TEXT SAFE AREA

Keep the left 42 percent of the frame consistently calm and readable for HTML headline, supporting text and CTA.

The left side may contain:
- slow forest parallax;
- subtle atmospheric haze;
- very faint ambient data particles.

The left side must not contain:
- the UAV;
- a bright scan beam;
- dense point-cloud activity;
- plot-boundary intersections;
- high-contrast carbon-map regions;
- flashing or rapid movement.

Keep all primary visual events within the center-right and right side.

MOTION CHARACTER

Use confident, clearly visible motion while preserving a professional technical tone.

Required motion:
- UAV forward translation;
- natural forward pitch;
- rotor motion blur;
- multi-layer forest parallax;
- camera tracking;
- gentle camera orbit;
- gradual camera elevation;
- moving LiDAR footprint;
- expanding processed survey swath;
- progressive point-cloud reveal;
- transition to a top-down geospatial view.

Motion must feel deliberate and survey-oriented, not aggressive or action-oriented.
